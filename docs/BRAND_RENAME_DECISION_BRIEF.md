# TaskMaster Pro brand rename decision brief

> **Status:** proposal for owner approval
>
> **Research snapshot:** 25 August 2026, approximately 14:30 EEST
>
> **Implementation status:** no application, service, identifier, or visual asset has
> been renamed
>
> **Primary recommendation:** **DayVector**
>
> **Proposed tagline:** **Turn direction into daily progress.**

## 1. Executive recommendation

Retire **TaskMaster Pro** as the public product name before a broad public launch.
The exact name is already in commercial use, the exact `.com` is registered, and
multiple products appear under the same or effectively identical name in the
productivity category. Keeping it would create avoidable confusion in search,
downloads, support, reputation, and future trademark work.

The best replacement from this naming round is **DayVector**.

DayVector communicates the product's actual value more accurately than another
task-oriented name:

- **Day** keeps the product personal, practical, and grounded in what the user can
  do now.
- **Vector** means direction plus measurable movement. That matches a system that
  connects roadmaps, daily execution, actual effort, reports, and coaching.
- The name can grow beyond a to-do list into health-aware planning, performance
  insight, focus tools, and other platforms.
- It is short enough for an icon label, clear in English, and reasonably easy to
  pronounce in Arabic and German.
- At the time of this research, `dayvector.com` and `dayvector.app` returned no
  public RDAP registration record. This is encouraging, but it is **not a
  reservation or a guarantee of registrability**.

### Approval boundary

This brief is a decision document, not authorization to start the rename.

No source code, package identity, cloud configuration, database, release artifact,
or existing logo should change until the owner explicitly approves one name and
identity direction. A valid approval can be as simple as:

```text
APPROVE: DayVector
TAGLINE: Turn direction into daily progress.
LOGO: Direction A
```

The owner can instead reply with `REFINE` and a desired direction such as warmer,
shorter, more premium, more Arabic-rooted, or more enterprise-oriented.

## 2. Why the current name should be retired

This is more than a domain inconvenience.

- A company identified as **TASKMASTER PRO TECHNOLOGIES INC** publishes a
  **TaskMaster Pro** Android application. Its Google Play listing shows more than
  5,000 downloads and points users to `taskmasterpro.com` for support.
  [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.taskmasterpro.evv)
- A different Android publisher describes another productivity application as
  **Taskmaster Pro**, with task management, voice input, AI assistance, and
  reminders—features close enough to create direct category confusion.
  [Second Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.taskmasterpro.app)
- A Chrome extension is also published as **TaskMaster Pro** and describes task
  categories, priorities, reminders, cross-device synchronization, and offline
  operation.
  [Chrome Web Store listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/taskmaster-pro/dcejecjgkehdhgcaikfolligbikpifbm)
- `taskmasterpro.com` has an active registration record and currently redirects to
  Statewise. The first Google Play listing still uses that domain as its product
  website.
  [Current domain destination](https://taskmasterpro.com/)

### Decision

The collision is exact, active, international, and present in the same broad
software category. Even if the products differ in depth or audience, users should
not have to work out which TaskMaster Pro is ours. A new brand is the correct move.

This is a brand and product-risk assessment, not a legal opinion. Domain ownership,
company-name registration, and trademark rights are different questions.

## 3. The product truth the new brand must carry

The repository describes the product as a local-first execution, roadmap,
activity-attribution, and evidence-based coaching application for Windows and
Android. The landing experience adds the clearest customer promise: turn goals into
roadmaps and daily actions, measure real effort, and improve through explainable
coaching.

The brand therefore needs to represent five connected ideas:

1. **Direction** — goals become practical roadmaps.
2. **Execution** — the user focuses on the right next action.
3. **Reality** — progress is informed by actual effort, not optimistic estimates.
4. **Adaptation** — plans and coaching respond to what happened.
5. **Trust** — local-first behavior, privacy choices, and reliable synchronization
   matter as much as features.

### Positioning statement

> For people managing meaningful work and long-term goals, DayVector is a personal
> planning and execution system that turns direction into daily action, connects
> actual effort to progress, and offers explainable guidance across Windows and
> Android.

### Brand personality

- Calm, not noisy
- Intelligent, not mystical
- Precise, not controlling
- Encouraging, not childish
- Capable, not corporate-heavy
- Privacy-conscious, not surveillance-oriented

### Naming territory to avoid

- `Task`, `todo`, `master`, `pro`, `AI`, or `smart` as the main differentiator
- Names locked to a timer, Pomodoro, calendar, or single feature
- Aggressive hustle language that conflicts with health-aware coaching
- Names that require a spelling explanation every time they are spoken
- Names whose exact `.com` leads to another software product

## 4. Naming method

The candidates were judged using the following weighted criteria. The resulting
scores are strategic judgments, not legal-clearance scores.

| Criterion | Weight | Question |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Product fit | 30 | Does it express direction, execution, real progress, and coaching? |
| Distinctiveness | 25 | Is it ownable and clearly different from generic task apps? |
| Domain and open-web signal | 20 | Is the exact `.com` apparently unregistered, with no obvious exact software collision? |
| English / Arabic / German usability | 15 | Is it pronounceable, memorable, and free of an obvious negative meaning? |
| Visual potential | 10 | Can it support a simple, recognizable icon and wordmark? |

The initial pool was filtered aggressively. Attractive names whose exact `.com`
already had a registration record were removed. Candidates with obvious software,
company, or product collisions were also removed.

## 6. Candidate identity briefs

### 6.1 DayVector — recommended

- **Pronunciation:** `DAY-vek-ter`
- **Arabic rendering when transliteration is helpful:** `داي فيكتور`
- **Public capitalization:** `DayVector`
- **Primary domain target:** `dayvector.com`
- **Defensive domain target:** `dayvector.app`

#### Why it fits

In mathematics, a vector has direction and magnitude. In this product, the roadmap
sets direction, the day's actions create movement, and measured effort shows the
magnitude. That gives the name a genuine product story rather than a decorative
metaphor.

The word `Day` prevents the analytical idea from becoming cold. It says the system
helps with today's reality while keeping the long-term direction visible.

#### Identity stack

- **Brand:** DayVector
- **Tagline:** Turn direction into daily progress.
- **Short promise:** Plan the path. Focus on today. Learn from what happened.
- **Draft store title:** DayVector: Plan, Focus, Grow
- **Descriptor:** Personal planning, execution, and evidence-based coaching
- **Voice:** clear, grounded, observant, respectful

#### Brand story

> A goal gives you a destination, but progress is made one day at a time. DayVector
> turns long-term direction into practical action, keeps focus connected across
> devices, and uses real effort to make the next plan more honest.

#### Risk and response

`Vector` is a technical word and also appears as a generic data term. That is not an
obvious competing product in the current open-web search, but it means the design and
copy should feel human. Rounded geometry, encouraging language, and the word `Day`
provide that balance.

### 6.2 PaceVector

- **Tagline:** Keep direction. Build momentum.
- **Story:** The user should not merely move faster; the user should move in the right
  direction at a pace that can be sustained.
- **Strength:** Strong fit for forecasting, workload, health awareness, and coaching.
- **Caution:** Without careful messaging it could be mistaken for a fitness or
  business analytics product.

### 6.3 EffortArc

- **Tagline:** Make today's effort move the bigger picture.
- **Story:** Small periods of real work accumulate into a meaningful long-term arc.
- **Strength:** Expresses the application's unusually strong connection between
  actual activity and roadmap progress.
- **Caution:** The word `effort` can sound like pressure. The experience would need
  to consistently include recovery, health, and respectful coaching.

### 6.4 GoalLoom

- **Tagline:** Weave goals into everyday progress.
- **Story:** Goals, phases, tasks, routines, resources, activity, and review become
  one coherent fabric.
- **Strength:** Warmest, most creative identity in the shortlist, with excellent
  visual potential.
- **Caution:** `Loom` also appears in phrases such as “a deadline looms,” and the
  metaphor is less immediate for some non-native English speakers.

### 6.5 DayBearing

- **Tagline:** Find your direction. Move the day forward.
- **Story:** The product helps the user get their bearings and choose a useful next
  action.
- **Strength:** Calm navigator-and-coach personality.
- **Caution:** Bearings are also mechanical components, weakening product-category
  clarity and search intent.

## 7. Current logo assessment

The current identity has a recognizable concept but should not be carried forward as
the main logo of a new brand.

Assets reviewed include:

- the 3840 × 1474
  [gold/black transparent main logo](../media/app-logo/TaskMaster_Pro_Black_Gold_Transparent_main-logo.png);
- the 512 × 512
  [compact blue/green hourglass mark](../media/app-logo/TaskMaster_Pro_Compact_Primary.png);
- the gold Android launcher treatment; and
- the simplified
  [hourglass/check SVG](../landing-page/assets/images/taskmaster-hourglass.svg) used
  by the landing page.

### What works

- The hourglass plus checkmark quickly communicates time and completion.
- The compact silhouette is recognizable at medium size.
- Existing light, dark, and gold variants show awareness of product themes.
- The landing-page SVG is cleaner and more adaptable than the rendered raster marks.

### What holds it back

- Hourglasses and checkmarks are common productivity symbols, so the mark is hard to
  own.
- It says timer and completion, but not roadmap direction, measured reality,
  adaptation, or coaching.
- Metallic bevels, glow, multiple gradients, and fine internal detail make the raster
  versions feel dated and visually noisy.
- Detail and color separation collapse in tray, notification, favicon, and small
  launcher contexts.
- The gold, blue, cyan, green, and white treatments do not yet behave like one
  disciplined identity system.
- `Pro` sitting below the current wordmark creates an awkward hierarchy and reinforces
  the generic-name problem.

### Verdict

Keep the **idea of time-aware progress**, but replace the hourglass/checkmark artwork
and old wordmark. The new symbol should represent **direction becoming progress**.

## 8. New logo design direction

### Direction A — Vector Day (recommended)

Create a flat, geometric symbol built from two ideas:

1. an open circular form that suggests a day cycle and subtly forms a `D`; and
2. one rising path that crosses the circle, creates a `V` in negative space, and ends
   in a directional point.

The mark should feel like a route becoming forward motion—not a checkbox placed on a
clock.

#### Construction imagination

- Start with a 24 × 24 grid.
- Use one consistent rounded stroke.
- Begin the path with a small origin dot in the lower-left quadrant.
- Let the path dip once to form the lower point of a subtle `V`.
- End in the upper-right quadrant with a clean directional cut, not a literal cartoon
  arrowhead.
- Use no more than two internal shapes.
- Make the monochrome silhouette recognizable before adding color.

#### Meaning

- Open circle: today is a cycle, not a prison.
- Origin point: present reality.
- Vector path: chosen direction.
- Rising endpoint: measurable progress.
- Negative-space `V`: the second half of DayVector without forcing a monogram.

### Direction B — Evidence trajectory

Use three rounded waypoints connected by a gentle rising curve:

- waypoint one: plan;
- waypoint two: focused action;
- waypoint three: observed result.

The final segment continues beyond the third point to show that review changes the
next plan. This direction is warmer and more narrative, but it must remain simple
enough to work at 16 pixels.

### Recommended palette

| Role | Color | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Foundation | Night Ink | `#101828` | Wordmark, dark surfaces, trustworthy base |
| Direction | Vector Indigo | `#4F46E5` | Primary brand action and symbol |
| Movement | Momentum Mint | `#20C6A4` | Progress accent; not body text on white |
| Attention | Signal Amber | `#F5B942` | Sparse milestones, warnings, and golden-theme bridge |
| Canvas | Cloud | `#F7F9FC` | Light brand background |

The core mark must also work in pure black and pure white. A restrained
indigo-to-mint gradient may be used in marketing artwork, but the product icon needs
a reliable flat-color version.

### Wordmark direction

- Use a humanist geometric sans with open counters and moderate rounding.
- Keep the public spelling `DayVector`; do not add `Pro`.
- Give `Day` and `Vector` equal weight. Color can separate them, but size should not.
- Test `D`, `V`, `a`, and `e` carefully at small sizes and in low-resolution Windows
  surfaces.
- Keep the Latin brand name globally. Arabic copy may add `داي فيكتور` where a
  pronunciation aid is useful, but it should not create a second translated brand.

### Things not to draw

- Another checkbox, hourglass, alarm clock, trophy, rocket, or target
- An AI sparkle, brain, robot, or magic wand
- A complex compass with cardinal letters
- A detailed roadmap that becomes visual noise
- A gold 3D badge as the only primary logo

### Required final asset system

The approved identity should eventually include:

- master vector symbol and wordmark;
- horizontal and stacked lockups;
- one-color black and white versions;
- light and dark digital versions;
- Android adaptive foreground/background and monochrome icon;
- Android notification icon;
- Windows application `.ico` at all required sizes;
- a truly legible one-color Windows tray icon at 16 pixels;
- favicon and social preview assets;
- splash artwork that does not depend on text; and
- clear-space, minimum-size, and incorrect-use guidance.

The product's existing golden theme can remain a user-selectable theme. It should not
force the new master brand to be metallic or gold.

## 9. Domain and Cloudflare direction

After owner approval and before any public announcement:

1. Run Cloudflare's definitive real-time check for the approved `.com` and `.app`.
2. Review registration and renewal prices.
3. Complete the trademark clearance step below.
4. Register the primary `.com` immediately if both checks are acceptable.
5. Register the `.app` defensively and redirect it to the primary domain.
6. Enable MFA on the Cloudflare account, registrar lock, DNSSEC, deliberate
   auto-renewal, and appropriate registrant privacy.

Cloudflare notes that a search result is not the source of truth; the final Check or
purchase flow confirms availability. Cloudflare Registrar domains also use
Cloudflare nameservers.
[Cloudflare Registrar documentation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/registrar/)

### Suggested hostname plan for DayVector

| Hostname | Initial role |
| --- | --- |
| `dayvector.com` | Canonical marketing, privacy, terms, contact, and download site |
| `www.dayvector.com` | Redirect to the canonical apex domain |
| `app.dayvector.com` | Reserve for a future web client or signed-in portal |
| `help.dayvector.com` | Documentation and support |
| `status.dayvector.com` | Service health when a public status service exists |
| `releases.dayvector.com` | Optional signed release delivery endpoint |

Useful addresses would include `support@`, `privacy@`, and `security@`. They should
not be advertised until inbound delivery and retention policies are configured and
tested.

## 10. Trademark and legal clearance gate

An apparently open domain does not make a name legally safe.

Before release, conduct a professional clearance search in the markets where the app
will be offered. At minimum, search:

- the exact word mark;
- spacing and capitalization variants;
- similar spellings and pronunciations;
- related software, productivity, coaching, health, and analytics goods/services;
- national or regional trademark databases relevant to launch; and
- company names, app stores, domain records, and ordinary internet use.

The USPTO specifically warns that an exact-word search is only a knockout search and
that a comprehensive clearance search should include similar marks, related goods or
services, state databases, and the internet. It also recommends considering a private
trademark attorney because clearance can be complex.
[USPTO federal trademark searching guidance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/federal-trademark-searching)

None of the shortlisted names is represented here as legally cleared or registrable
as a trademark.

## 11. Safe implementation strategy after approval

The rename should be a compatibility migration, not a global search-and-replace.

### Pre-brief impact snapshot

A repository scan performed before adding this document found the old public or
technical identity on **967 matching lines across 199 files**, excluding build,
package-cache, Git, and release-output directories. The largest groups were:

| Area | Matching files |
| --- | ---: |
| Tests | 90 |
| Flutter application code | 33 |
| Supabase migrations and templates | 26 |
| Android | 11 |
| Landing page | 10 |
| Assets / release notes | 7 |
| Tools | 6 |
| Windows | 5 |

Many matches are tests, compatibility identities, or immutable migration history.
They should not all be edited simply because they contain the old name.

### Surface-by-surface policy

| Surface | First-release policy | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visible app title and copy | Rename | This is the customer-facing brand |
| Logos, icons, splash, website | Replace | Identity must be coherent everywhere |
| Localization strings | Rename and re-review EN/AR/DE | A visible old-name fragment is a functional defect |
| Windows ProductName / FileDescription / installer display | Rename | These are public installed-product surfaces |
| Android label, widget label, notification text | Rename | These are public device surfaces |
| `pro.taskmaster.app` application ID | **Preserve by default** | Changing it can create a different Android app and break upgrade continuity |
| `pro.taskmaster.app://auth-callback` | **Preserve initially** | Existing OAuth configuration and installed clients depend on it |
| Existing Android signing key | **Preserve** | Required for upgrade continuity |
| Drift database names such as `taskmaster_<user-id>` | **Preserve or explicitly migrate** | A blind rename can make existing local data appear lost |
| Secure-storage keys and vault key aliases | **Preserve or dual-read/migrate** | Data-access and device-key continuity are critical |
| Notification channels, alarm identities, widget/provider classes | Preserve or version with migration | Installed OS state may retain the old identifiers |
| Supabase project reference and migration history | **Preserve** | Project identity and applied history are not branding copy |
| Activity self-detection identities | Recognize both old and new names | Prevent the renamed app from being counted as productive user activity |
| GitHub repository and internal Dart identifiers | Optional later cleanup | They do not need to block the public brand migration |

If the app has never been publicly distributed and the owner explicitly chooses a
new package ID, that should be a separate decision with a tested account, database,
auth, signing, deep-link, widget, notification, and upgrade plan.

### Implementation phases

#### Phase 0 — reserve and clear

- Approve one name, tagline, and logo direction.
- Run the definitive Cloudflare check and register the domains.
- Complete formal trademark clearance.
- Confirm store display-name availability.

#### Phase 1 — centralize the public identity

- Introduce one authoritative brand configuration for visible name, short name,
  tagline, website, support email, and legal links.
- Route UI and release tooling through it where appropriate.
- Do not rewrite historical migrations or compatibility identifiers.

#### Phase 2 — replace the visual system

- Produce the approved vector master.
- Generate Android, Windows, tray, notification, web, social, and splash variants.
- Visually inspect each asset at its real rendered size in light, dark, and golden
  themes.

#### Phase 3 — migrate public surfaces

- Update Flutter UI, localizations, notification copy, widgets, reports, exports,
  email templates, Windows metadata, installer text, landing pages, privacy/terms,
  release descriptions, and support links.
- Keep explicit legacy aliases wherever installed state or user data depends on the
  old technical identity.

#### Phase 4 — prove continuity

Verification must distinguish source tests from installed-product proof:

- full source analysis and automated tests;
- Windows and Android clean-install checks;
- installed upgrade from the last TaskMaster Pro build to the new brand;
- same account, tasks, roadmaps, settings, vault access, timers, reminders, widgets,
  and synchronization after upgrade;
- Google sign-in and callback behavior;
- notification and background-action behavior;
- live Cloudflare domain, redirects, TLS, legal pages, downloads, and support email;
- final packaging only after the last source or asset change.

#### Phase 5 — release and transition

- Publish one coordinated release across app, installer, website, and notes.
- Explain once: “TaskMaster Pro is now DayVector.”
- Avoid running two public identities indefinitely.
- Preserve legacy technical aliases for as long as installed upgrades require them.

## 12. Final recommendation to approve

Approve the following direction:

```text
NAME: DayVector
TAGLINE: Turn direction into daily progress.
POSITION: Personal planning, execution, and evidence-based coaching
DOMAIN TARGET: dayvector.com
DEFENSIVE DOMAIN: dayvector.app
LOGO: Direction A — Vector Day
PRIMARY COLORS: Night Ink, Vector Indigo, Momentum Mint
```

It is the strongest balance of product truth, distinctiveness, domain signal,
international usability, visual potential, and room to grow.

Approval should authorize the next planning and implementation stage—not a blind
rename. The first action after approval should be the definitive Cloudflare domain
check and legal-clearance gate, followed by a compatibility-preserving migration.
